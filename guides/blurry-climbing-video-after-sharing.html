Video quality troubleshooting

Why Your Climbing Video Looks Blurry After Sharing

Find where climbing footage loses detail: compare the original, saved edit, and shared copy before changing crop, export, or upload settings.

Published November 3, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

Find the first version that loses detail

If a climbing clip looks sharp in your editor but soft in a message or feed, do not immediately re-film the attempt or blame the camera. Compare three versions: the original recording, the saved export, and the copy played in the destination app. The first step where visible detail disappears tells you which part of the workflow to investigate. This guide is about image quality after sharing, not tracking accuracy or missing trim boundaries.

Make a fair comparison before changing settings

Choose one recognizable moment, such as a stable position before the crux. Compare the same frame or short sequence in each version at a similar displayed size. Look at hold edges, clothing detail, and the outline of a moving hand. A paused frame and normal playback answer different questions: a single moving hand can be blurred even when the rest of the image is clear. Comparing a full-screen original with a tiny feed preview can also hide the actual difference. Keep a note of the source file and export you used.

If the original is already soft

Check whether the whole scene is soft, only moving parts smear, or the climber is simply very small in a wide shot. These are different symptoms; they do not prove a specific camera fault. Exporting at a larger resolution cannot recover detail that was never recorded. For your next ordinary session, clean the lens, confirm focus on the climbing area, and test the composition in the available light. Keep phones and tripods outside landing zones and follow gym rules. Do not repeat a risky move just to replace a recording. The filming guide covers the broader setup.

If the original is clear but the saved edit is soft

Open the exported file outside the editor. If that file is already worse, investigate the edit before uploading again. A tight digital crop uses a smaller part of the original picture, so an aggressively enlarged climber can expose limited source detail. Compare a wider version of the same sequence. If that looks better, reduce the crop or accept a smaller subject instead of assuming that another export setting will fix everything. See the vertical-crop guide for keeping useful body and wall context.

If your editor offers output controls, check the saved dimensions and the chosen quality setting rather than relying on a preset's name. Change one setting at a time and export a short test from the original project. A larger file is not automatically better, and this guide does not prescribe one universal resolution, bitrate, or frame rate. Keep a clear reference export and avoid making each new edit from a previously shared copy. These are general troubleshooting steps, not a claim that CruxCut exposes every export control.

If only the shared copy looks worse

Upload the same reference file directly to the intended destination rather than downloading it from a chat and uploading that copy again. Sharing services may process or compress video, and previews may differ from the file you saved. Let the upload and any visible processing finish, then play the actual posted or sent item. If a quality option is available in that service, check its current setting; names and behavior can change, so do not assume a setting from another app applies.

For a useful test, keep the source and destination constant. Change only the transfer path or one relevant setting, then compare the same moment. If playback quality varies between devices or connections, record that observation rather than concluding the source file changed. A cover image, an editor preview, or one brief low-detail playback does not verify the finished shared video. You do not need to post repeated public duplicates to test: use a private or limited-visibility option when the service provides one and that suits your consent requirements.

A practical example: the climber looks sharp, then blocky

Suppose the original and saved export both show clear hold edges, but the sent copy breaks into visible blocks around a moving climber. That points you toward the sharing or playback stage, not automatically toward a camera or tracking problem. Try sending the unchanged reference file through a different supported transfer path, or compare the destination's available playback quality options. If the saved export already has those blocks, return to the edit instead. This is a diagnostic example, not a measured CruxCut failure or a promise that a particular platform setting will solve it.

Keep the review copy separate from the social copy

A tightly cropped social clip can be useful for viewing the action, while a wider original can be better for checking foot placements and surrounding holds. Keep both. Label the original, reference export, and shared copy so you can reproduce the comparison later; the video-organization guide provides a simple system. For movement analysis, use the clearest available source and follow the beta-review workflow rather than interpreting missing pixels as a certain hand or foot movement.

Before you troubleshoot again

  • Compare the same moment at a similar display size.
  • Identify the first soft version: original, saved export, or shared playback.
  • Test a wider crop if the saved edit enlarges a small subject.
  • Change one export or transfer choice at a time, using the original project.
  • Keep the reference file and respect permission to share identifiable people.

If you need support, describe which version first loses detail, the device and editor used, and the destination service.
