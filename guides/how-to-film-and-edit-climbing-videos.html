How to Film and Edit Climbing Videos

Better climbing clips start with a stable shot, clear framing, and an edit that keeps the movement—not the waiting.

Published August 26, 2026 · By the cruxcut team

This practical starter guide is for climbers who want better gym or outdoor clips without turning every session into a production day.

1. Start with the shot, not the edit

The easiest way to make a climbing video look better is to fix framing before you press record. Most editing pain comes from clips that are too far away, too shaky, or cut off the start or finish.

  • Keep the full problem or route visible, including the start holds and finish.
  • Leave extra room above the top-out or clipping stance.
  • Lock the camera off when possible. A stable wide shot is easier to edit than a drifting handheld clip.
  • Leave room for a 9:16 crop if the final clip is for Reels, Stories, or Shorts.
  • Roll a few seconds early and late so the setup, first move, and reaction are not lost.

2. Film for the climb you want to remember

Climbing clips work best when they communicate one clear idea: the crux move, the full send, the beta sequence, or the fall that explains why the route was hard.

3. Keep the edit simple

Most climbing videos do not need heavy effects. They need less dead time and a clearer story.

  1. Trim the walk-up, chalk break, and long rest unless they add context.
  2. Choose one focus: a full send, one move, or a short sequence of attempts.
  3. Put the strongest movement first when editing for social.
  4. Use short on-screen text only when it explains the climb, grade, or move.
  5. Export for the destination you use. A 9:16 result fits Reels and Shorts.

4. When an AI climbing video editor helps

Manual editing is fine for one simple clip. It gets slower when the original footage is wide, shaky, or long.

  • Climber auto-tracking helps when the athlete looks tiny in a fixed wide shot.
  • Move-based cuts make it easier to review a sequence instead of scrubbing through the whole attempt.
  • Highlight selection helps turn the best moments into one shareable recap.

6. Quick checklist before you post

  • The full climb is visible from start to finish.
  • The climber stays readable on a phone screen.
  • The clip starts quickly and removes dead time.
  • Captions do not cover the hands, feet, or crux.
  • The export matches the channel you plan to use.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best camera position for filming climbing?

Use a stable position that keeps the climber, start holds, feet, and finish in frame. Leave extra room above the finish and start recording a few seconds early.

Should climbing videos be filmed vertically or horizontally?

Choose based on the destination. A stable wide source preserves the full climb for review; a 9:16 vertical result fits Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.

How can I make a climbing video follow the climber?
