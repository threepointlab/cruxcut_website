Safer camera placement

How to Film Climbing Without Blocking the Landing Zone

Choose a climbing-camera position without putting a phone, tripod, or filmer in a fall zone. Check gym rules, traffic, framing, and consent before recording.

Published November 10, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

Get permission and choose the safe position first

A useful climbing video is not worth putting equipment or another person where someone could land. Before choosing a dramatic angle, check your gym's filming rules and ask staff where a phone or tripod is permitted. Rules and layouts differ. This guide is a filming checklist, not a substitute for the facility's safety instructions or a universal safe-distance rule.

If you cannot record from a permitted position outside the relevant fall and movement areas, skip the recording. Do not ask a climber to change their fall or landing to protect your phone. A wide shot from an allowed location is better than a close shot that introduces an obstacle.

Look beyond the start holds

Stand back and inspect the whole problem, including the finish, sideways movement, possible swings, nearby problems, and the path people use to walk around the mat. The starting position does not define the entire area a climber may occupy or fall into. A route that traverses can bring a climber much closer to a camera than the first move suggests.

  • Check the landing and movement areas associated with the whole problem, not only the patch beneath the start.
  • Look for adjacent climbers and overlapping problems. Your own attempt is not the only activity to consider.
  • Keep exits, walkways, access to holds, and staff-designated clear areas free of equipment.
  • If the acceptable location is unclear, ask staff before placing anything. Do not calculate a supposedly safe radius from a video guide.

Do not treat the crash mat as a camera shelf

A small phone, a low tripod, a bag used as a support, or a charging cable can still be an obstacle. Being below knee height does not make an object safe to land on. Do not hide equipment at the edge of a mat or behind someone else's belongings and assume other climbers will notice it.

Use only a support and location allowed by the gym. Do not attach a mount to holds, walls, rails, or other facility equipment without permission. Keep cables out of the climbing and walking areas. If there is no suitable permitted support, ask a non-climbing companion to film from an allowed location or leave the attempt unrecorded.

Frame the full movement from the allowed position

Once the position is acceptable, check what the camera actually sees. Include the starting stance, likely hand and foot positions, sideways movement, and the finish. Leave margin rather than filling the screen with the climber at the start. A tighter crop can be considered later, but missing source footage cannot be recovered by zooming out in an editor.

Record a brief framing check without occupying or obstructing another climber's attempt. Review the saved clip for visible feet, hands, and finish holds. If a wide lens makes the climber too small, consider another permitted position or keep a wider review clip. Do not move equipment into the landing area merely to improve detail. Our filming and editing guide covers framing choices, while the vertical-crop checklist helps assess the later edit.

Give filming its own person and attention

A person actively belaying should not operate a phone or divide attention between belaying and recording. Follow the gym's belay procedures and use a separate filmer if filming is permitted. Similarly, do not assume that someone can provide spotting or other safety attention while concentrating on a screen. Roles that require full attention come first.

A separate filmer should stay in an allowed position and remain aware of nearby climbing and foot traffic. Do not walk backward across the mat while watching the screen, follow underneath a climber, or step into another landing area to keep a dynamic move in frame. If the view becomes blocked, let the shot be blocked.

Recheck between attempts

A setup that was acceptable a few minutes ago may no longer be appropriate when the gym gets busier or another problem is in use. Before each recording, look away from the screen and check the surrounding activity again. Move or remove equipment when staff ask, when traffic changes, or when the intended filming location is no longer clear.

  1. Confirm that recording is allowed and the equipment is still in the permitted place.
  2. Check nearby climbers, landing areas, and access routes.
  3. Confirm the whole intended movement is visible from that position.
  4. Stop filming if keeping the shot would require entering a restricted or unsafe area.
  5. Collect the equipment when you finish rather than leaving an unattended filming station.

Separate permission to record from permission to share

Follow the gym's policy and ask the people you intentionally film before recording or posting identifiable footage. Be particularly careful around children and private conversations. Someone agreeing to help film your attempt does not automatically mean everyone visible in the background has agreed to a public reel.

Review the saved recording before sharing. If other people or private details are visible, choose a different segment or do not publish that version. Cropping can change the frame, but it is not a guarantee of anonymity or permission. Keep a useful personal review clip without turning every recording into a public post.

Edit after the environment is clear

Move to a permitted rest area before reviewing footage or editing. Do not sit in a landing zone with your attention on the phone. Preserve the original and choose the part of the attempt that explains the movement; see where to trim the start and finish and how to review climbing beta.

Editing tools can help present a recording, but they do not make an unsafe camera setup safe. The priority order is simple: facility rules and climbing safety, consent and awareness, complete source framing, then the edit. If those first requirements cannot be met, leave the camera packed away.
