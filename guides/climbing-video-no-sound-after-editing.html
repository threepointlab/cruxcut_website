Missing audio troubleshooting

Why Your Climbing Video Has No Sound After Editing

Find where climbing footage loses its sound. Compare the original, editor preview, saved file, and shared copy before changing audio or exporting again.

Published November 6, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

Find the first silent copy before changing the edit

If a climbing clip has no sound, first check whether it was recorded without audio, muted during editing, or silenced only in the player or sharing preview. Those are different problems. Keep the unchanged original and compare it with the actual saved edit, not only the preview inside an app.

This guide is a general troubleshooting workflow. It does not claim that CruxCut has an audio-repair tool, separate audio tracks, or a particular export setting. A silent preview alone does not establish an app bug.

Check playback volume with a known audible clip

Play a short video that you know contains sound in the same player. Check the player's mute button and the device's media volume. If you use headphones or a wireless speaker, check where audio is being sent. Disconnecting an accessory briefly can help isolate an unexpected output route.

Do not rely on the phone's ringer setting alone: media playback and notification volume may be controlled differently. If both the known audible clip and the climbing clip are silent, investigate playback first. If the known clip is audible, move on to the original climbing recording.

Compare four stages using the same moment

Choose a moment that should contain a recognizable sound, such as your own spoken countdown. A quiet gym section may be genuinely quiet, so absence of an obvious noise is not enough. Keep playback volume moderate rather than turning it up sharply.

  1. Original: Open the camera recording outside the editor and listen to the selected moment.
  2. Editor preview: Listen to that same moment in the project, if preview audio is available.
  3. Saved file: Open the actual exported file in your gallery or another available local player.
  4. Shared copy: Compare the destination post or downloaded message with the saved file.

Write down the first stage where the expected sound disappears. If the original is audible but the saved file is not, that narrows the issue to the editing or saving path. It does not identify the exact cause. If the saved file has sound but the destination does not, re-editing the original may not help.

If the original is already silent

Make a new, separate test recording with a brief spoken phrase. Check that it plays back audibly before filming another attempt. If the recording app offers a microphone permission or recording-audio setting, inspect its current state. Permission granted now does not prove that audio was captured in an older file.

A player being silent cannot by itself prove that the file has no audio track. When necessary, share a permitted sample with support for file inspection. If audio was never captured, raising a volume control cannot recreate the original sounds. Keep the climbing footage and use a clearly intentional silent edit or a permitted soundtrack instead of pretending the original sound was recovered.

If sound disappears during editing or saving

In an editor that exposes audio controls, check clip mute, track volume, and any export option that excludes sound. Inspect only controls that actually exist in your version; do not assume every editor has them. Adding music can also change what you hear, so compare source sound and added music separately where the editor allows it.

Run one short test from the original with no added music and no deliberate audio changes. Save it as a new file and listen outside the editor. Avoid changing crop, speed, video quality, and audio together, since that makes the result harder to interpret. Keep the original and the previous export until you have a verified replacement.

If timing is also wrong, use the playback-speed checks. If the chosen moment was removed by a trim, review the start and finish boundaries before diagnosing missing audio.

If only the shared copy is silent

Check the destination player's mute state and the post's visible audio status. Some sharing flows offer a mute toggle; a platform may also show an audio restriction notice. Read any actual notice instead of assuming a copyright claim from silence alone. Compare your own saved file with the posted version before uploading again.

Music available inside one platform is not automatically licensed for exporting and reposting elsewhere. Use original audio you are allowed to share or verify the soundtrack's rights for the intended platform and commercial use. Background music captured in a gym may also create rights questions; recording it incidentally does not establish reuse permission.

If you also lose visual detail after sharing, diagnose that separately with the sharing-quality checklist. Do not repeatedly export or publish copies while you are still locating the problem.

A useful support report takes one short sample

  • State whether the original, preview, saved file, and shared copy are audible.
  • Name the device, app version, recording mode, and players you tested.
  • Identify whether all sound is missing or only original audio or added music.
  • Include the exact tested moment and any visible restriction or error message.
  • Send only footage you are permitted to share. Obtain consent for identifiable people and avoid private conversations in the background.

A screen recording can demonstrate what you heard during playback, but it may capture sound differently from the underlying file. Keep the source and output files available for permitted inspection. Use a clear original-versus-edit naming system so support can compare the right pair.
