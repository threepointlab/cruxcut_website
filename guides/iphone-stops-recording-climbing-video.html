Recording troubleshooting

iPhone Stops Recording Mid-Climb? Check the Saved Clip First

Diagnose an interrupted climbing recording without deleting your footage. Check the saved ending, temperature warnings, storage, and a short camera test.

Published November 17, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

Start with what was actually saved

You finish an attempt, return to the phone, and discover that the recording ended before the top. Before changing an editor, open the original in Photos and play its final seconds. Note the saved duration, the point where it ends, and any message the phone showed. Keep that original even if it is incomplete.

If the original includes the finish but an edited copy does not, this is a different problem. Check the trim boundaries and compare the saved export. An editor cannot recover movement that the camera never recorded; a shorter export does not prove the camera stopped.

1. Treat a temperature warning as a reason to stop

Apple explains that prolonged camera use in hot conditions or direct sunlight can affect device temperature, and that camera features may be temporarily disabled when the device gets too warm. That is a possible explanation to investigate, not proof that heat caused a particular recording to end.

If the iPhone displays a temperature warning, follow Apple's temperature guidance: turn it off, move it to a cooler environment away from direct sunlight, and allow it to cool. Do not keep restarting the recording to bypass a warning. A warm phone without a warning is not by itself evidence of overheating; Apple also describes normal warmth during camera use.

For an outdoor session, choose a shaded, permitted filming position when practical. Do not move a phone into a landing area, walking route, or belayer's working space to keep it out of the sun. If there is no safe place to film, skip the recording. See the safe filming-position checklist.

2. Read any storage or camera message before changing settings

Check available device storage in Settings > General > iPhone Storage. Record what the phone reports rather than assuming a short clip means storage was full. There is no universal free-space threshold for every resolution, frame rate, device, and recording length.

If space is limited, move verified copies of files you no longer need on the phone through your normal backup workflow. Confirm that a copy opens before removing anything. Do not delete the only copy of a climbing session, or assume that a cloud thumbnail means the full original is safely available elsewhere. Our iCloud-original checklist explains that separate issue.

3. Run a short, saved test before the next attempt

After addressing any warning, use the same camera app, lens, and video mode you intend to use. Record a short test from a safe position, stop it yourself, and play the saved file through to the end. Confirm that both picture and sound are present. A moving preview or timer is not the same as a verified saved recording.

If that test succeeds, try a modestly longer test during a normal rest period. Keep the phone attended and out of other people's way. A successful short test does not guarantee that an entire long session will record reliably. Avoid asking someone to repeat a demanding climb solely to diagnose the camera.

If you choose to compare a lower resolution or frame rate, change only one setting and compare saved tests. This can help you isolate a condition; it is not a guaranteed cure, and no particular mode is required by this guide. Keep the original settings noted so you can restore your preferred setup.

4. Separate recording failure from an app-specific problem

If a third-party camera repeatedly stops but the built-in Camera app records the same short test, preserve both results. Note the camera app and version, iOS version, video mode, duration, storage reading, and the exact warning or error. That comparison is useful for support but does not establish a software bug or rule out differences between recording modes.

If the built-in Camera also stops, avoid repeatedly recording long attempts as a test. Follow the device maker's current support guidance. Do not reset the phone, remove apps, or discard footage simply because the cause is unclear.

Keep a tiny incident log

  • Original saved duration and the last visible move.
  • Whether the phone stopped, the app closed, or only the export was shorter.
  • Exact warning text, if any; do not infer a warning from warmth alone.
  • Camera app, lens, resolution, frame rate, and approximate session conditions.
  • Available storage and results of one short saved test.

This small log is more useful than changing several settings at once. Once a complete original is available, move on to beta review or make a separate sharing edit. Preserve the original until the exported copy is checked.

Scope and source
