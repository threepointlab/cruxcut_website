Climbing Video Stuck in iCloud? Download the Original Before Editing

Troubleshoot an iCloud climbing video that will not download or import. Check the original, connection, and device storage without deleting your only copy.

Published November 12, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

A thumbnail is not proof that the full video is on your phone

You find yesterday's climbing attempt in Photos, but an editor cannot open it, or Photos keeps trying to load it. If you use iCloud Photos with Optimize Storage, your phone may keep a space-saving version while the full-resolution original remains in iCloud. That is one possible explanation, not a diagnosis of every import failure.

Start in Photos rather than repeatedly importing the same clip. Can you play the whole attempt and scrub from the starting stance to the finish? If the original does not load there either, first investigate the download. If it plays fully in Photos but fails only in one editor, move on to the editor's access, format, or import troubleshooting. Do not assume auto-tracking or cropping caused a failure before editing began.

1. Protect the original before changing anything

Do not delete the recording to force a fresh download. Apple states that deleting content with iCloud Photos enabled also removes it from iCloud and other devices using iCloud Photos. A synced library is not an independent safety copy. Do not empty Recently Deleted as an experimental fix.

If you have another copy on a computer or external drive, open it and check the entire attempt before relying on it. A visible filename or thumbnail alone does not verify a usable copy. Avoid signing out of iCloud, turning off synchronization, or reinstalling an editor as your first step, especially if the editor holds unfinished local projects.

2. Try one clip on a reliable connection

Use a reliable Wi-Fi connection and open the particular climbing video in Photos. Give the download time to finish, then try full playback and scrubbing again. If you are at a gym with weak connectivity, wait until you have a dependable connection rather than repeatedly starting the same import between attempts. Downloading originals may also use cellular data when allowed; check your plan and preferences before relying on it.

Test a short, recently recorded local video as a comparison. If that opens in the editor while the older cloud-backed attempt does not, the difference is useful troubleshooting evidence, not proof of a specific app bug. Note the error wording and whether the failure occurs in Photos, the file export, or the editor.

3. Check device storage separately from iCloud storage

Free space in iCloud and free space on the iPhone are different. Check Settings > General > iPhone Storage for device capacity. An available cloud plan does not guarantee enough room to download a large original, create an editing copy, and save an export locally.

There is no universal free-space multiplier for every video editor. Inspect the actual file size when available and leave room for additional working files. Move independently backed-up, nonessential items first. Do not remove the only copy of a climbing session or delete an app containing unsaved projects just to make the warning disappear. If the phone remains nearly full, postpone the edit or work on a verified copy using another device.

4. Make a deliberate copy when needed

Apple documents downloading selected photos and videos with Photos > Select > Share > Export Unmodified Originals, then choosing a destination in Files and saving. Wording can vary by system version. Choose a supported destination with enough space, such as an external storage device if you use one. Exporting to a cloud-backed Files location is not the same as guaranteeing an offline local file.

After the copy finishes, check its duration, beginning, middle, and finish. Keep the original intact. Only import the file if your editor supports that import route; this guide does not claim that every editor, including CruxCut, accepts every Files location or video format. You do not need to download your entire photo library to troubleshoot one attempt.

5. If Photos works but your editor still cannot import

  • Confirm you selected the intended attempt, not an incomplete copy or a different version.
  • Check whether the editor has access to that specific video under the permissions provided by your version of iOS. Do not grant broader access than you need just as a guess.
  • Record the editor version, iOS version, clip duration, and exact error. Do not publish private footage or account details in a support request.
  • Try one known-good local clip. If only one recording fails, preserve it for diagnosis rather than repeatedly converting or deleting it.

A completed download does not establish format compatibility, successful analysis, or a correct export. Check each stage separately. Once you can edit, preserve the full-body source and use our vertical framing checklist. If the saved edit is fine but the sharing copy is too large, that is a different problem covered in making a smaller sharing copy.

Before leaving for an offline session

Open the attempts you want to review while you have a connection, confirm they play fully, and keep a verified accessible copy where appropriate. Test access without a connection before depending on it at a gym or crag. Do not assume an item remains downloaded indefinitely when storage is optimized.

For comparing attempts once the footage is available, see reviewing climbing videos for beta. Download availability, useful beta footage, and a polished social edit are separate goals.

Sources and scope

This checklist applies to iPhone recordings stored with iCloud Photos, not every Android cloud service or every failed CruxCut import. Apple's official documentation explains iCloud Photos and Optimize Storage, downloading originals and synchronized deletion, and device versus photo storage. Checked November 12, 2026. The troubleshooting order and climbing examples are editorial recommendations, not measured recovery rates or a promise that a particular download will succeed.
