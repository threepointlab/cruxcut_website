How to Edit a Bouldering Video on Your Phone (Beginner Steps)

Start with the original, trim to the attempt, choose the shape you will post, frame the climber, check speed and sound, then export and watch the saved file.

Published November 25, 2026 · By the CruxCut team

The short version: six steps in order

Editing a bouldering video does not have to be complicated. If you are starting from a raw clip on your phone, work in this order, because each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Keep the original. Save an untouched copy before you change anything.
  2. Trim to the attempt. Cut the waiting, keep the setup and the finish.
  3. Pick the shape you will post. Tall, square, or in between.
  4. Frame the climber. Zoom or follow so the body stays readable.
  5. Check speed and sound. Make sure nothing plays in slow motion by accident.
  6. Export, check, and file it. Watch the saved file, not the preview, then name it so you can find it.

Step 1: Keep the original file

Before you edit, make sure the video is fully on your phone. A clip that is still stored in the cloud can export badly. If the file looks stuck, our guide on downloading the original from iCloud before editing walks through it. Then edit a copy or export to a new file so the original stays untouched. You can always start again from the original if a crop or a trim does not work out.

Step 2: Trim to the attempt

Bouldering clips usually have a lot of standing around at the start and a long tail at the end. Cut those, but keep a moment of setup so viewers can see where the climber starts, and keep the finish or the fall so the outcome is clear. Trimming too tightly is the most common beginner mistake, because the clip feels abrupt. Our guide on where to trim a climbing video shows how to choose the start and end points.

Step 3: Pick the shape you will post

Decide where the clip is going before you crop. A tall 9:16 shape suits Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. A square 1:1 shape or a 3:4 shape can fit a normal feed post. Climbing is tall, so a tall shape often feels natural, but it also cuts off the sides of the frame, which is where an outstretched hand or foot can disappear. The guide on cropping vertically without cutting off hands or feet covers this trade-off. If your clip came out sideways, fix that first with this rotation guide.

Step 4: Frame the climber

If the climber looks small, you have three options. You can leave the clip wide, use one fixed crop, or use a crop that follows the climber up the wall. Which one fits depends on how far the climber moves in the frame. For a short boulder problem a single crop often works. For anything taller, a moving crop is better, and you can set it by hand or let a follow-cam tool do it. Our guide on zooming in on a climber after filming compares these options, and the guide on auto-tracking problems explains what to do when a tracker loses the climber.

Step 5: Check speed and sound

A phone clip can pick up an odd playback speed or lose its sound after editing, and it is easy to miss on a small preview. Play the clip once from start to finish at normal speed. If it plays in slow motion when you did not choose that, read why a climbing video plays in slow motion after editing. If the sound disappeared, read why a climbing video has no sound after editing. Speed changes are useful on purpose, for example to replay a hard move, but only when you chose them.

Step 6: Export, check, and file it

Export a new file, then open the saved file and watch it. The preview inside an editor and the exported file can look different, and a shared copy can look softer still. If a shared version looks blurry, see why a climbing video looks blurry after sharing. If it is too large to send, see how to make a smaller copy. When you are happy with it, name it by problem and attempt so you can find it later, as in how to organize climbing videos by problem and attempt.

Do you need a climbing-specific app?

Not for the first four steps on a simple clip. Your phone's own tools are usually enough to trim and to change the shape. A climbing-specific app is most useful for step 4, where it can find the climber and move the crop for you. Beginner checklist before you post

  • Did you keep the original recording?
  • Does the clip show where the climber starts and how the attempt ends?
  • Are hands and feet still visible in the shape you chose?
  • Does the climber stay in frame from start to finish?
  • Does the exported file play at the speed and with the sound you expect?
  • Is the file named so you can find this attempt later?

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to edit a bouldering video on my phone?

Keep the original file, trim the clip to the attempt, choose the shape you will post, make sure the climber stays framed, and export a copy. Many phones can trim in the built-in video player or gallery. A climbing-specific app can also follow the climber for you, but you still need to review the result.

Should I edit the original video or a copy?

Work from the original recording whenever you can and export a new file. Each time a clip is shared or re-compressed it can lose detail, and an untouched original lets you try a different edit later.

What shape should I export a bouldering video in?

Choose the shape for where you will post. A tall 9:16 shape fits Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. A square 1:1 or a 3:4 shape can suit feeds. Before you commit, check that hands and feet are still visible in the shape you picked.

Do I need a special app to edit climbing videos?

No. A basic trim in your phone's own tools is enough for many clips. A climbing-specific app mainly helps when you filmed from a tripod and want the crop to follow the climber, which is slow to do by hand.
